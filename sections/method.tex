\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\method{} alternates environment research and student learning (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). A fixed Planner maintains research context and issues bounded decisions; separate workers implement complete environments. Neither role may change the trainer, official evaluator, or sealed test data. The current representation replaces the earlier mandatory World--Scene hierarchy with a complete \code{Environment} interface.

\subsection{Research State and Objective}
\label{sec:object}

Let $\pi_{\theta_t}$ be the student, $o_t$ its optimizer state, $\mathcal B_t$ a versioned environment library, $\mathcal C_t$ an evidence-linked requirement map, and $\mathcal M_t$ maintained research experience. A plan $P_t$ selects program revisions, sampler parameters $\eta_j$, and explicit task allocations $n_j$. Its realized training distribution is
\begin{equation}
q_t(x)=\sum_{j\in I_t}\frac{\widetilde n_j}{\sum_{k\in I_t}\widetilde n_k}
q(x\mid E_j^{v_j},\eta_j),
\label{eq:distribution}
\end{equation}
where $I_t$ contains accepted, explicitly allocated environments and $\widetilde n_j\le n_j$ is the actual distinct-task count after construction. There is no implicit replacement allocation when an environment fails. The first library is empty; generic interface code and neutral examples are supplied, not a benchmark-specific task bank.

For fresh tasks $D_t$, the fixed training procedure $\mathcal U$ collects new online episodes and continues the student:
\begin{align}
(\theta_{t+1},o_{t+1})&=\mathcal U(\theta_t,o_t,D_t), \label{eq:update}\\
F_{t+1}&=\operatorname{Eval}_{\rm dev}(\pi_{\theta_{t+1}}), \nonumber\\
P_{t+1}&=\operatorname{Plan}(F_{t+1},L_t,\mathcal B_{t+1},\mathcal C_t,\mathcal M_{t+1}), \label{eq:evolve}
\end{align}
where $L_t$ is version-linked formal-training feedback. These equations describe heuristic research decisions, not a differentiable meta-objective or a guarantee of monotonically improving scores. The trained student does not replace the Planner.

\subsection{Source-Linked Diagnosis and Measured Requirement Groups}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

Permitted development trajectories are submitted for analysis with their public instructions, tool schemas, calls, observations, later requests, and official outcomes. Unavailable analyses are disclosed; original cases and scores remain accessible. Analysis distinguishes task demands, required environment mechanisms, and consequential agent behavior. Successful examples supply useful strategies; failures supply observed mistakes and qualified expected behavior. A failed task alone does not establish which capability is missing, and an agent-invented prerequisite must not become a task rule.

Reports are merged bottom-up. At every node, the model jointly reconciles domain names and summarizes their environment, task-behavior, and failure-behavior content. Source-item references are retained, and unique trajectory counts are computed by the controller rather than generated by the model. Compact summaries lead back to complete permitted cases and cross-checkpoint histories. Source coverage makes an interpretation inspectable; it does not prove that the summary preserved every important condition.

Separately, the Planner maintains stable requirement IDs with natural-language definitions and sets $J_c$ of real development-task IDs. The controller computes their observed performance:
\begin{equation}
s_t(c)=\frac{1}{|J_c|}\sum_{i\in J_c}
\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} y_{t,i,k},
\label{eq:capability}
\end{equation}
where $y_{t,i,k}$ is the recorded task outcome. Definitions can be revised with provenance, in which case baseline and current outcomes are recomputed on the same membership. Groups may overlap; task success is not an isolated measurement of every requirement in a composite task. This map supplies a more stable comparison than counts under freely changing summary labels.

\subsection{Persistent Planning and Selective Library Evolution}
\label{sec:planning}

The Planner inspects current versus previous and baseline development results, source cases, the environment catalog, and per-environment online reward distributions. Training observations are associated with the code revision and sampler parameters that produced them. They are not fresh measurements of a revised environment or the final policy.

For each chosen environment the action is \emph{create}, \emph{revise}, \emph{retain}, or \emph{pause}. Creation may cite a parent; revision preserves identity and publishes new code; retention uses the existing implementation with supported sampler parameters; pausing disables sampling while retaining code and history. Omitted environments receive no tasks that round. Horizontal expansion adds missing mechanisms; vertical adaptation changes dependencies, information burden, constraints, or instance workload. Either can simplify a task, but should not remove the decision that motivates it.

Every registered requirement receives an explicit train, monitor, or defer decision and a rationale. A train decision requires an associated positive allocation; this is an auditable planning contract, not a guarantee of semantic coverage or retained competence. The studied planning budgets allow at most ten create/revise decisions per round and one to ten instances per selected environment, with a total target of 96. Retain decisions do not consume this planned-edit budget; subsequent defect repair remains bounded.

The Planner maintains compact, revisable experience: what was attempted, what was observed, where a lesson applies, and what remains uncertain. Its model and session persist across research turns, while its written experience is corrected rather than treated as an infallible log. The current decision-brief variant additionally records expected effects and explicitly reviews whether prior interventions landed and transferred. This stronger review contract is present in the native-ACE study but not the earlier two studies (Appendix~\ref{app:versions}). An observed score increase is still an association, not a causal attribution to one program edit.

\subsection{Complete Executable Task Environments}
\label{sec:environment}

An environment is a complete module with a deterministic task sampler, episode construction, executable tools, and a reusable verifier. \code{make\_task(seed, parameters)} fixes a request and JSON payload. \code{start(task)} creates independent mutable state and actor-visible interfaces from that payload. \code{check(task, state, trace)} evaluates the actual outcome. A Task binds the exact environment version, payload, sampling settings, and initial-information fingerprint. Reset does not call a model or resample a goal.

The same concrete goal data determines the request and checking target. Tools declare their schema and implementation together; the framework checks argument consistency, actor ownership and dynamic availability, records actual receipts, and restores state on declared rejection. User-owned actions execute on the same episode state, with private receipts disclosed only through legitimate interaction. Scripted later requests and model-backed users are both supported. State may be a database, virtual filesystem, or ordinary JSON structure; a separate business World is not required.

The studied cohort uses programmatic Boolean or component-based \code{Score} rewards. Component weights and mandatory guards are fixed before rollout; strict success requires all required outcomes and guards, whereas numeric reward can recognize partial progress. Checks should accept lawful alternative routes and distinguish a real effect from a completion claim. Shared data and typed execution narrow inconsistency risks but cannot prove arbitrary generated programs correct.

\subsection{Bounded Construction, Online Learning, and Feedback}
\label{sec:checks}

Implementation workers receive the plan, relevant analysis, prior code when available, maintained experience, and interface examples. They emit a complete module, a change summary, and limitations. Candidate environments undergo isolated CPU checks of reconstruction, initial information, state isolation, tool effects, and verifier behavior. An independent model screens representative concrete instances for material executability or agent-side discoverability problems. Each candidate permits at most one code repair; accepted candidates are published independently. An unchanged, previously accepted code/parameter pair can reuse its review, but its new tasks still receive executable checks.

There is no teacher solution requirement, separate cold-start phase, pre-training student probe, or reward-variation admission gate. The final promoted library must reproduce the checked task semantics. Duplicate or failed candidates create explicit shortfalls, not replacement tasks from unplanned environments. A positive smaller batch is allowed, including a partial final optimizer batch; if no distinct accepted tasks remain, training is not submitted.

Formal RL generates eight episodes per Task in the reported studies. Rewards are normalized within the original task group even when episodes are segmented for training. Student-generated tokens, including retained reasoning tokens where applicable, determine policy loss; environment/user observations do not. Both weights and optimizer continue from the last completed training boundary. Fixed-development evaluation then supplies new evidence, while sealed test outcomes remain operator-only. Online all-one, all-zero, constant-partial, and mixed groups inform subsequent planning; none alone establishes task validity or useful transfer.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\method{}: persistent research over executable environments}
\label{alg:envopt}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Student $(\theta_0,o_0)$, permitted dev evidence, generic interfaces/examples, budgets
\State Initialize empty library $\mathcal B_0$, requirement map $\mathcal C_0$, and experience $\mathcal M_0$
\For{$t=0,\ldots,T-1$}
  \State Analyze current dev trajectories; merge with source links
  \State Publish measured requirement groups and versioned training feedback
  \State Persistent Planner inspects evidence; writes actions, allocations, and maintained experience
  \State Implement selected create/revise actions; check all selected candidates with bounded repair
  \State Publish accepted versions; construct exactly allocated distinct Tasks $D_t$
  \If{$D_t=\varnothing$}
    \State Stop with retained failure evidence; do not submit training
  \Else
    \State Continue $(\theta_t,o_t)$ by online RL on $D_t$; evaluate fixed dev
    \State Preserve the completed program, task, learner, and feedback boundary
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
