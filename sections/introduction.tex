\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) asks whether AI systems can use evidence from one development cycle to improve the next. MetaRSI\textcolor{magenta}{[CITE: MetaRSI]} distinguishes three writable surfaces for such improvement: data, the execution harness, and model parameters. Combining the data and model surfaces creates a data--model loop, and recent data agents have begun to automate its data side. Autodata~\citep{autodata} iteratively synthesizes and refines examples using feedback from fixed weak and strong solvers, DataMaster~\citep{datamaster} searches and transforms external data under downstream-training feedback, and RSIBench-Data~\citep{rsibenchdata} studies agents that revise training-data strategies across training runs. Together, these works show that agents can discover useful data strategies, but RSIBench-Data also exposes a discovery--reliability gap: agents often fail to improve those strategies consistently across rounds. This gap raises a sharper question: when the learner itself is updated, can feedback from one training round reliably guide data construction for the next?

For tool-using models, this question extends beyond selecting or generating static examples because the training data are executable: they must implement state, tool effects, tasks, and outcome checks. Systems for executable environment synthesis and scaling, including AgentScaler, EnvScaler, ScaleEnv, Agent World Model, and EnvFactory~\citep{agentscaler,envscaler,scaleenv,awm,envfactory}, show that such resources can be constructed automatically. We study a stateful multi-round online-RL setting built on this capability. Starting with no supplied executable training library, target backend, or verifier implementation, a fixed researcher agent constructs training programs for the current target policy $\pi_t$; a fixed online-RL procedure produces $\pi_{t+1}$; and training records and new behavior from $\pi_{t+1}$ guide the next data round. The loop therefore crosses and carries forward the model update itself: each updated policy becomes both the starting point and the feedback source for the next round. Our focus is the evidence required to decide what to construct or revise as the target policy changes. Here, the constructed instances specify what training experience can occur, while the current policy determines which trajectories and rewards are actually realized during RL. Reliable improvement therefore requires each training target to remain connected to the behavior that motivates it, the experience actually realized during training, and the behavior of the updated model.

Closing this loop around a training target is difficult at three handoffs. First, behavioral evidence must be distilled into a training target without losing the requirements and failure mechanisms that justify it. Second, the selected target must be faithfully realized as executable training data. Third, the intended data must remain connected to the experience that actually reaches RL and to the targeted behavior observed afterward. Breaks at any of these handoffs leave the next data decision without reliable evidence, even when generation, training, and aggregate evaluation all complete successfully.


We introduce \method{}, a framework that closes the data loop around each training target. First, a \emph{source-traceable behavioral map} combines per-trajectory analysis with hierarchical consolidation, coverage checks, and links back to the task requirements and failure mechanisms that motivate target selection. Second, \emph{specification-driven construction and refinement} translates a selected target into explicit success, failure, and recovery cases; persistent builders implement the corresponding state, tools, interactions, and verifier, and refine them through independent review. Third, \emph{target-linked feedback} connects the declared target and expected effect to versioned training instances, actual RL groups and rewards, and subsequent target-model behavior. Together, these mechanisms make the motivation for a training target traceable, its executable realization inspectable, and its training feedback available for joint interpretation in the next round. Hierarchical merging, record organization, program generation, and review are implementation mechanisms for this loop rather than separate algorithmic claims.




Across four Qwen3-4B tool-use studies, the loop produces nonmonotonic training trajectories and validation-selected checkpoints. The clearest matched private-evaluation result is BFCL, which improves by 16.67 percentage points; ACEBench-Agent improves by 11.11 points, with a paired interval that includes zero. The $\tau^2$ run regresses after an intermediate peak, ACEBench fluctuates across rounds, and later reward saturation in the Codex-Vanilla runs illustrates why a best checkpoint and sustained recursive improvement are different outcomes. Separately, a frozen-trajectory diagnostic shows that the refined behavioral map retains more task and failure mechanisms in shorter reports. This diagnostic evaluates analysis quality rather than the cause of the training gains, and the current single-seed studies do not isolate the contribution of each component.


\paragraph{Contributions.}
We make three contributions. First, we formulate from-scratch data-centric recursive improvement for online agent training by distinguishing executable data construction from the on-policy experience realized during RL, and identify three breaks around a training target: incomplete behavioral understanding, incomplete executable realization, and disconnected data--training--evaluation feedback. Second, we introduce \method{}, which addresses these breaks through source-traceable behavioral analysis, specification-driven construction and refinement, and target-linked feedback. Third, we provide an end-to-end study across four tool-use benchmarks, process diagnostics of behavioral coverage, training exposure, and learning signal, and an implementation-fidelity case study. The results support evidence-guided data evolution while also exposing the remaining gap between discovering a useful checkpoint and improving reliably across rounds.
