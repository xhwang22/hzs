\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Data-centric recursive self-improvement (RSI) places an agent in the role of a data researcher: inspect a model's behavior, construct training experience, train the model, and use the resulting feedback to decide what to try next. RSIBench-Data studies this cross-model research setting with a fixed researcher and controlled training and evaluation services~\citep{rsibenchdata}. Its discovery--reliability gap motivates looking beyond whether an agent can find a useful dataset once. The harder question is how it should conduct the next round of research as both the model and its training data change.

We study this question for tool-using models, with permitted target tasks and interaction trajectories but without access to the target backend or verifier implementation. The training-environment library starts empty. The researcher must construct independent executable environments that supply state, tool effects, new task instances, and outcome checks. The target model trains in these environments with online RL, and its updated evaluation trajectories inform the next environment-generation round. This is a from-scratch environment-research constraint, not an absence of model priors, generic interface examples, or evidence from the public eval set. Executable synthesis and environment adaptation already provide substantial foundations~\citep{envscaler,awm,envharness,spade,rods}; our focus is how to organize the evidence that guides their repeated use.

Across this workflow, we identify three challenges: \emph{incomplete behavioral analysis} can omit task requirements and failure mechanisms; \emph{disconnected data, training, and evaluation} obscure which intervention was actually tested; and \emph{incomplete environment implementation} can omit mechanisms required by the training target. Section~\ref{sec:evidencegaps} examines these through report-coverage measurements, a training-exposure case, and a generated-program audit. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} previews the two evidence gaps addressed by our core designs.

We present \method{}, organized around two complementary designs. A \emph{traceable behavioral map} combines per-trajectory analysis, hierarchical merging with source links, and measured task-linked requirement groups. The data researcher can survey behavioral patterns and return to the original cases, rather than treating a compressed interpretation as ground truth. \emph{Target-conditioned context refinement} targets an \emph{information-utilization gap}: access to feedback does not ensure its use in research decisions. Target-linked feedback organizes intended training, actual RL experience, and subsequent target outcomes around a shared behavioral target to form \emph{decision-ready context}.

A persistent data researcher owns the research interpretation and expected effects. Parallel, persistent Env Builders implement its specifications and examples, refining their programs through independent review. This execution workflow realizes the two designs; program generation, environment memory, and validation are not claimed as separate inventions. We also do not treat the bottlenecks as a measured universal failure of native coding agents: the relevant comparison must match information access and research and training budgets.

The training evidence comprises preliminary three-benchmark Qwen3-4B studies of an earlier implementation. BFCL improves substantially, while $\tau^2$ regresses after an intermediate peak and ACEBench fluctuates on a small private eval set. These observations motivate tracking the full research trajectory rather than reporting only the best checkpoint. They do not yet isolate the two designs or validate the latest persistent-builder revision; those claims require the controlled comparisons specified in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. The separate analysis diagnostic evaluates report quality on frozen Qwen3-8B trajectories, not the cause of these training gains.
